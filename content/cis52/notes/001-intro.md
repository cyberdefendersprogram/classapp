## Intro to Cloud Computing

### What is cloud computing?
Cloud computing is the on-demand delivery of compute power, database storage, applications, and other IT resources through a cloud services platform via the internet, with pay-as-you-go pricing.

The simplest definition: the "cloud" is a computer located somewhere else that you access and use via the internet. "Web services" is another name people use for the cloud.

### Cloud is somebody else's computer
The cloud is made up of server computers in large data centers around the world. When you use a service like AWS, you're using computers owned by AWS — a cloud services provider.

Those computers offer building-block technology features and services you can assemble into solutions that meet your business and technology needs. With cloud computing, organizations consume on-demand compute and storage rather than building, operating, and improving their own infrastructure.

### Cloud vs On-Prem
Cloud computing lets you stop thinking of infrastructure as hardware and start thinking of it (and using it) as software.

Before cloud computing, you had to provision capacity based on guessing theoretical maximum peaks. Under-provision and you fall short of demand; over-provision and you pay for expensive resources sitting idle.

### On-Premise Infrastructure Challenges
Hardware solutions are physical, which means they require:

- Space, staff, physical security
- Planning and capital expenditure
- Guessing at theoretical maximum peaks
  - Is there enough resource capacity?
  - Do we have sufficient storage?
  - What if your needs change? You have to spend the time, effort, and cost to change all of this.

Objective:
Students will investigate a major cybersecurity breach of their choice from the last 10 years to understand its broader implications ("forest") and technical specifics ("trees"). This assignment encourages critical thinking, practical application of security frameworks, and concise communication.

Part 1: Written Paper Submission

Length: 3-5 pages (excluding references)

Paper Structure:

  1. Introduction (10%)

    • Brief overview of the case study (e.g., Equifax, Stuxnet, SolarWinds, Colonial Pipeline, etc.).
    • Highlight the breach’s significance and why it was chosen.
    • State the objectives of the paper: understanding both the "forest" and "trees."

  2. Background (20%)

    • Describe the victim organization or target.
    • Contextualize the breach in terms of industry, geopolitical relevance, or technological landscape.

  3. Attack Lifecycle Analysis (30%)

    • Detailed analysis of the breach using the MITRE ATT&CK framework or similar security models.
    • Discuss Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) used by attackers.
    • Highlight key Indicators of Compromise (IOCs) and how they were identified or could have been detected.
    • Explain any vulnerabilities exploited and methods used (e.g., spear-phishing, supply chain attack, zero-day vulnerabilities).

  4. Impacts and Aftermath (20%)

    • Analyze the broader impact: financial, reputational, regulatory, or societal.
    • Summarize legal and regulatory changes (if any) that followed.
    • Discuss lessons learned and industry changes triggered by the breach.

  5. Recommendations and Mitigations (15%)

    • Suggest practical actions to prevent or mitigate similar breaches.
    • Propose detection strategies students can use in the lab (e.g., writing detection rules, monitoring specific logs).

  6. Conclusion (5%)

    • Summarize findings and highlight the interplay between understanding the big picture (forest) and mastering technical details (trees).

  7. References (Mandatory)

    • Cite all sources using APA or MLA format.

Part 2: Class Presentation

Length: 5 minutes
Presentation Date: Final Class

Presentation Structure:

  1. Introduction (1 minute):

    • Briefly introduce the breach and why it was significant.

  2. Key Findings (3 minutes):

    • Discuss the TTPs, IOCs, and notable lessons learned.
    • Highlight how the breach changed security practices or policy.

  3. Takeaway (1 minute):

    • Share one key insight students can apply in practice (e.g., use of a specific MITRE technique or real-world detection example).

Tips for Presenters:

  • Use visuals or diagrams to explain complex ideas (e.g., MITRE heatmaps, attack timelines).
  • Practice to stay within the time limit.
  • Be ready to answer questions from peers or instructors.

Suggested Case Studies:

  1. Equifax (2017): Exploitation of unpatched Apache Struts vulnerability leading to PII compromise of 147 million people.
  2. Stuxnet (2010): Nation-state malware targeting Iranian nuclear centrifuges; illustrates cyber-physical attacks.
  3. SolarWinds (2020): Supply chain attack affecting numerous organizations and U.S. government agencies.
  4. Colonial Pipeline (2021): Ransomware attack causing critical infrastructure shutdown.
  5. Uber (2022): Multi-factor authentication fatigue attack compromising systems and revealing organizational weaknesses.
  6. Capital One (2019): Cloud misconfiguration breach exposing 100 million credit applications.

Grading Rubric

Paper (100 points):

  • Depth of analysis: 40%
  • Clarity and organization: 20%
  • Use of MITRE framework and technical depth: 20%
  • References and citation: 10%
  • Grammar and presentation: 10%

Presentation (50 points):

  • Content clarity and conciseness: 50%
  • Use of visuals and examples: 30%
  • Engagement and delivery: 20%

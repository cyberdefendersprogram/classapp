The assignment

Instead of re-reading an old breach, research one new company that works on incident response: finding, investigating or containing attacks faster. Say what it sells, what data it reads, where it uses AI (what the company claims versus what it shows), what you could build yourself with the tools from this course, and what you would ask in a demo. This connects Class 3 (what a sensor can and cannot see, including encrypted traffic) to Bejtlich Ch. 9–11 (collection, analysis, escalation and resolution) and to the alert-handling lesson from Target.

Choose a company

Pick one from the company list on the CIS 53 portal Tools page, “Incident Response Startup Case Study”. The list covers two kinds of company: AI SOC and autonomous investigation (for example 7AI, Dropzone AI, Prophet Security, Qevlar AI, Surf AI, Torq) and cloud and SaaS incident response and forensics (for example Mitiga, Cado Security, Ciroos). How to research

  • Start with the company’s own site, documentation, blog and demo videos. Then add press releases, customer stories, conference talks and independent coverage.
  • Funding figures in roundups conflict. Confirm every figure in a primary source (the company’s announcement or a filing) and cite where you found it, with the date.
  • Treat marketing as claims, not facts. Say what the company says and what it shows.
  • Where sources disagree (funding, dates, features), say which you trust and why. Say “possibly” for anything the sources do not establish, and name who says so.
  • Check every link opens and says what you claim. A made-up or mismatched citation costs points.
  • Do not sign up for trials, share school or personal data with a company, or contact the company on the course’s behalf without checking with me first.

Template headings (100 points)

  1. Summary (5): company, product, buyer, your judgment in one sentence.
  2. The problem and the data it reads (10): the pain point, and a table of data sources (packets, flow, logs, alerts, cloud, identity, endpoint, email, AI/LLM logs) with what the product does with each. Say how it handles encrypted traffic.
  3. How it works and where AI is used (25): a table of at least four claims, the evidence shown for each, and the source. What is the model doing, and what does a human still decide?
  4. Build it yourself (20): which capabilities you could build with Wireshark, Zeek, Suricata, Sigma and a SIEM, what stays manual, and one concrete detection sketch with what it would miss.
  5. ATT&CK and ATLAS mapping (15): two behaviors the product says it catches, each mapped to a technique ID and name at attack.mitre.org (or atlas.mitre.org for attacks on AI), with what data would show it.
  6. Business and stage (5): funding, customers and competitors, each from a primary source.
  7. Critique (15): three demo questions, one risk (false positives, blind spots, data privacy, over-trusting the AI, or lock-in) and how you would check it, plus one sentence tying the company to Class 3: which of DNS, HTTP, TLS metadata or server-side evidence would it read, and what does it lose when traffic is encrypted?
  8. Sources (5): at least four, each with author or organization, exact title, date, link and access date, including at least two primary sources from the company and one independent source. Wikipedia can help you start, but it does not count.
  9. AI use disclosure (required, not graded): list any unedited or lightly edited AI text and attach your prompts (an appendix is fine). You own every fact, figure and ATT&CK ID.

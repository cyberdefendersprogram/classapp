Case study · Traffic to 103.165.181.11

Blocked cert or dead port?

Chrome distrusted a certificate authority, eMudhra, so a TLS error was the obvious guess. Two real captures show how to tell where a connection fails, layer by layer.

Finding

From this network, emudhradigital.com (103.165.181.11, owned by eMudhra) never answers the first TCP packet on port 443. No handshake completes, so no certificate is sent and the Chrome block is not the cause. For a real certificate failure we used untrusted-root.badssl.com, a site built for this.

Every packet goes out and none comes back. A closed port would answer at once with an RST, so silence points to a filter or an unreachable host. Wireshark filter: tcp.analysis.retransmission

What each capture tells a sensor

  • Capture 1: only IP addresses, a port and a SYN. No names, no certificate, nothing at layers 5–7.
  • Capture 2: the server name in the Client Hello, the certificate chain, and the alert. This is enough to log who tried to reach what and why it failed.

The eMudhra background

  • eMudhra is a certificate authority. Chrome blocked six of its intermediate TLS keys on Oct 17 after a key-generation flaw produced weak keys.
  • Chrome plans to remove the parent root from its trust store from Oct 27 Pacific.
  • eMudhra's own sites (emudhra.com, emsign.com and others) use Sectigo certificates, so they load fine. A site served under a blocked emSign intermediate would show the capture 2 pattern.

AI on the wire: live script

About 12 minutes. Three beats: what an AI call looks like in a capture, what an LLM does well and badly when it reads a capture, and how an attacker could use the same tools. Uses the captures from Demos 3 and 5, so nothing new to download.

01Show an AI call on the wire (3 min). Use Demo 3.

Open class2-filters.pcap, display filter:
tls.handshake.extensions_server_name
Say

Everything inside is encrypted, but the server name in the ClientHello is not. That is how a sensor knows this host talked to an AI provider without reading a word of the prompt. Ask: what can you tell, and what can you not tell, from this one line?

Expected answer

You can tell who talked to which service and when. You cannot see the prompt, the response or what data left. That is why the egress allowlist and the proxy log matter more than the packet.

02Show why AI traffic looks odd (3 min). Use Demo 5.

Open scan-beacon-noise.pcap
Statistics > Conversations > TCP, sort by Bytes
Then: ip.addr == 203.0.113.20
Say

This one connection is the biggest in the file: 402 packets of a few hundred bytes from the server, about 33 seconds. A long streaming response looks like exfiltration or C2 to a naive rule. Streamed LLM answers are the new false positive.

Contrast

Now show the beacon: ten-packet connections to 203.0.113.50:8443 every 60 seconds. Same destination type, completely different shape. Shape beats size.

03Let an LLM read the capture, live (4 min).

tshark -r scan-beacon-noise.pcap -q -z conv,tcp | head -40
tshark -r scan-beacon-noise.pcap -Y 'tcp.flags.syn==1 && tcp.flags.ack==0' \
  -T fields -e frame.time_relative -e ip.src -e ip.dst -e tcp.dstport | head -60
Paste into an LLM with this prompt

"You are a SOC analyst. Below is a summary of a 20-minute capture. List anything suspicious, give the evidence (host, port, timing), say how confident you are, and say what you would check next. Do not guess about anything you cannot see."

What to point out

It should find the 10.0.0.77 SYN scan (1,024 ports) and the 60-second beacon from 10.0.0.5 to 203.0.113.50:8443. Check every claim against the packets: ask it for frame numbers or timestamps and verify one in Wireshark. If it names a CVE, malware family or attacker you cannot see in the data, call that out as a hallucination. Only paste synthetic or sanitized data into a public AI service; real captures contain addresses, hostnames and sometimes credentials.

04Flip it: AI as the attacker (2 min).

Ask the room: if this beacon were generated by an LLM, what would you change?
Answer

Add random jitter, vary payload sizes, use a popular HTTPS API as the destination, and send at human-like hours. The regular 60-second interval is the easy case. Defenders need per-host baselines, destination allowlists and process-level telemetry, not just interval checks.

05Close with the three-part frame.

Threats TO AI  ·  Threats FROM AI  ·  Defending WITH AI
Say

TO: someone attacks the model or its data pipeline. FROM: attackers use AI to scale and disguise. WITH: we use AI to triage, but a human verifies every claim. Tie back to Lab 1: your evidence chain is what makes an AI's answer trustworthy.

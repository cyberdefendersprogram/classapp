Course description

CIS 53 teaches you to detect, investigate and explain intrusions using network traffic, IDS/IPS alerts, flow records, host and application logs, and threat intelligence. You work hands-on with Wireshark, tcpdump, Snort/Suricata, Zeek and a SIEM, and you finish with a multi-level investigation challenge.

Our approach: detect threats to, from, and with AI

Intrusion detection now includes AI systems. Alongside the classic network-forensics foundation, each week connects the skills to three questions:

  • Threats to AI: How do attacks on LLM applications and AI agents (prompt injection, tool abuse, data exfiltration through an agent) show up in network traffic and logs?
  • Threats from AI: How do AI-enabled attackers (automated reconnaissance, convincing phishing, faster tooling) change what defenders see?
  • Detection with AI: Where can AI assist an analyst, and how do you check its work against evidence?

The fundamentals come first: you cannot detect abnormal AI traffic until you can read normal traffic. The AI material replaces older content rather than adding to your workload.

Student learning outcomes

By the end of the course you will be able to:

  1. Explain the role of network monitoring and intrusion detection in defense in depth.
  2. Capture and analyze traffic with Wireshark, tcpdump and related tools, and interpret Ethernet, IP (v4 and v6), TCP, UDP and common application protocols.
  3. Identify suspicious traffic, indicators of compromise and attacker behavior, including in encrypted traffic using metadata.
  4. Explain the strengths and limits of IDS, IPS, HIDS, SIEM and network security monitoring; read and write detection rules; and triage alerts.
  5. Correlate network, host and application evidence with threat intelligence and map behavior to MITRE ATT&CK and MITRE ATLAS.
  6. Describe how attacks on and by AI systems appear in telemetry, and evaluate AI-assisted analysis critically.
  7. Design a monitoring architecture and document findings clearly and professionally.

The Final: Intrusion Detection Challenge

The Final is a multi-level, evidence-based investigation, with each level unlocked by what you found in the one before. Scenarios include both classic network intrusions and compromised AI agents: you work from packet captures, logs, alerts and tool-call records to establish what happened, which systems and data were affected, how the attacker succeeded, and what would have detected or stopped it.
